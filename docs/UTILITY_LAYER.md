# The shared cross-position dynasty utility layer

The four position engines rank players within their own position. They say nothing comparable
about anyone at another position, and each of their specifications says so explicitly. This
layer is what turns four within-position orderings into one dynasty board.

It imports no position engine, no inference module and no market module, and those boundaries
are enforced by `src/utility/boundary.test.ts`.

## The question it answers

> How much fantasy production would a roster lose by replacing this player with the one it could
> start instead?

That question has one answer per league format, it is measured in a unit every position shares,
and it is the only formulation under which a quarterback and a tight end can be compared at all.

## 1. League schema — `src/utility/leagueSchema.ts`

`DYNASTY_SUPERFLEX_12`, version 1:

| field | value |
|---|---|
| teams | 12 |
| dedicated | QB 1, RB 2, WR 3, TE 1 |
| flex slot A | count 2, eligible RB/WR/TE, allocation RB .45 / WR .45 / TE .10 |
| flex slot B | count 1, eligible QB/RB/WR/TE, allocation QB 1.0 |
| scoringId | `ppr-1.0` |
| nflTeams | 32 |

`DYNASTY_1QB_12` is the identical object with flex slot B removed. It exists so the Superflex
quarterback premium can be demonstrated rather than asserted.

Scoring is part of the schema because it changes what players are worth. A generated production
curve records the scoring it was built under, and `assertScoringMatches` refuses to value a
schema against a curve built under different rules.

### What is no longer in the schema

Version 1 carried `nflStartersPerTeam` — a hand-declared count of NFL starters per team at each
position (QB 1.0, RB 1.5, WR 3.0, TE 1.5). Those four numbers set every position's value ceiling,
and nothing supported them. Measuring the same quantity from box scores gives QB 0.91, RB 1.16,
WR 1.59, TE 0.44: the receiver figure was nearly double the truth and the tight end figure more
than three times it, both in the direction that compressed those positions.

The field is gone rather than corrected, because the measured production curve removed the need
for it. `derivedSupply()` still reports the quantity, from games, for diagnosis.

## 2. Production curve — `src/utility/productionCurve.ts`

Generated by `npm run generate:production-curves` from ingested nflverse regular-season box
scores. The shipped reference is `production-v1-2017-2025`: nine seasons, 5,439 player-seasons,
full PPR.

Two arrays per position:

- **`pointsPerGameByRank`** — the median, across seasons, of what the *n*th-best player at that
  position produced per **team game**.
- **`ageSurvival`** — the share of the position's total production that was produced above each
  age.

### Why points per team game, and why no minimum-games filter

Scoring per game *played* requires a minimum-games threshold, or one 40-point relief appearance
becomes the best season at the position. That threshold then truncates the curve wherever it was
set, flooring the bottom of the distribution far above zero and handing career backups a share of
an elite player's production they never earned — with a six-game minimum, the quarterback curve
bottomed out at 8.95 points per game.

Dividing by team games has neither problem and needs no threshold: missing eight games costs a
player half his value, exactly as it costs the roster starting him. Team games per season are
counted from the schedule the box scores describe, so the 16-game seasons before 2021 and the
17-game seasons after are each divided correctly.

Measured curve, shipped reference:

| pos | depth | rank 1 | rank 12 | rank 24 | rank 36 | rank 48 | rank 72 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 71 | 24.20 | 16.21 | 11.33 | 4.99 | 1.53 | 0.04 |
| RB | 156 | 23.61 | 13.53 | 10.69 | 8.21 | 6.29 | 3.20 |
| WR | 214 | 22.52 | 14.58 | 12.38 | 10.19 | 8.67 | 6.08 |
| TE | 123 | 17.48 | 8.56 | 6.03 | 4.12 | 2.85 | 1.42 |

Two things the old straight line could not express are visible here: the same rank is worth very
different amounts at different positions (RB3 against TE3), and the step down from the top costs
far more than the same step lower down.

Fumbles are absent from the scoring because the ingested weekly resource carries no fumble
column. The omission is worth roughly a fifth of a point per game to a high-volume back, is the
same for every player at a position, and changes no ranking.

## 3. Replacement — `src/utility/replacement.ts`

```
demand(pos)   = (dedicated[pos] + Σ slot.count × slot.allocation[pos]) × teams
replRank(pos) = clamp(round(demand), 1, curveDepth(pos))
replProd(pos) = productionAtRank(pos, replRank)
ceiling(pos)  = productionAtRank(pos, 1) − replProd(pos)
```

| pos | demand | repl rank | repl prod | elite prod | ceiling | median starter surplus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 24.0 | 24 | 11.33 | 24.20 | 12.87 | 4.89 |
| RB | 34.8 | 35 | 8.54 | 23.61 | 15.08 | 3.53 |
| WR | 46.8 | 47 | 8.68 | 22.52 | 13.85 | 3.71 |
| TE | 14.4 | 14 | 8.02 | 17.48 | 9.46 | 2.40 |

Every ceiling is now the distance between two measured points on a curve. In version 1 it was a
consequence of an assumed player-pool size.

## 4. The value — `src/utility/dynastyValue.ts`

```
production    = productionAtRank(pos, engineRank)
surplus       = max(0, production − replProd(pos))
proximity     = clamp(production / eliteProd(pos), 0, 1)
optionality   = AGE_FLOOR + (1 − AGE_FLOOR) × ageRunway(pos, age)
depth         = WEIGHT × medianStarterSurplus(pos) × proximity × optionality
total         = surplus + depth
value         = 100 × total / max(total over the board)
```

`DEPTH = { WEIGHT: 0.25, AGE_FLOOR: 0.5 }`.

The layer consumes **rank only** from a position engine. A composite's magnitude never leaves
the engine, because four numbers drawn from four distributions were never on a common footing.

Normalization happens after utility. The 0–100 scale is PlayerTicker's own and is deliberately
not mapped onto any market's units.

### Why the depth term exists

Strict value over replacement is right and, alone, unusable: it valued 86% of the live board at
exactly zero. A dynasty manager trades those players constantly, and a board that cannot tell a
22-year-old one rank below the line from a 31-year-old fifth receiver is not describing what they
are trading.

It is a discount on the valuation already here, not a second model. A player's depth value is a
fraction of what a median starter **at his own position** is worth, scaled by how close his
production comes to the best at his position and by how much of that position's career is still
ahead of him. Applying it to every player rather than only below replacement keeps the value
monotone and continuous across the replacement line; applying it only below would make the first
non-starter worth suddenly more than the last starter.

Measured behaviour on the live board: max depth is 7.3% of max surplus, and the best
below-replacement player sits at overall rank 115, far beneath any genuine starter.

### Why age appears once, not twice

Each position engine already carries an age-and-development component with real weight — a
quarter of the receiver dynasty composite. The rank this layer consumes has therefore already
been discounted for age, so applying a career horizon to a player's **surplus** would charge him
for his age twice. `AGE_FLOOR = 0.5` bounds the same double-count inside the depth term, where
age is doing different work: a 31-year-old backup and a 22-year-old backup at the same rank do
not hold the same optionality, and rank alone cannot say so.

## 5. Why the Superflex premium emerges

One line produces it: `allocation: { QB: 1 }` on the superflex slot.

| | 1QB | Superflex |
|---|---|---|
| QB demand | 12 | 24 |
| QB replacement rank | 12 | 24 |
| QB replacement production | 16.21 | 11.33 |
| **QB ceiling** | **7.98** | **12.87** |

The two schemas differ in that slot and nothing else. There is no positional multiplier and no
bonus anywhere in the code.

## 6. Sensitivity

Run against the live 868-player board.

**Supply** — no longer an input. In version 1, moving the declared WR supply from 3.0 to 2.0 cut
the WR ceiling by 43%. It now changes nothing, because no value reads it.

**Replacement convention** — this is the load-bearing assumption now. `k` scales demand: `k = 1`
means "the last weekly starter", `k > 1` means "the last rostered player".

| k | QB rank/ceiling | RB | WR | TE | top-25 QB/RB/WR/TE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 | 19 / 10.9 | 28 / 13.8 | 37 / 12.5 | 12 / 8.9 | 6 / 7 / 10 / 2 |
| **1.0** | **24 / 12.9** | **35 / 15.1** | **47 / 13.8** | **14 / 9.5** | **8 / 7 / 9 / 1** |
| 1.2 | 29 / 16.0 | 42 / 16.5 | 56 / 14.9 | 17 / 10.1 | 11 / 7 / 6 / 1 |
| 1.5 | 36 / 19.2 | 52 / 17.9 | 70 / 16.4 | 22 / 10.8 | 13 / 6 / 6 / 0 |

**Curve window** — two disjoint five-season halves, to show the curve is not an artefact of one
era.

| curve | QB | RB | WR | TE | top-25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017–2025 (shipped) | 12.87 | 15.08 | 13.85 | 9.46 | 8 / 7 / 9 / 1 |
| 2021–2025 | 13.45 | 13.80 | 15.06 | 9.09 | 10 / 6 / 8 / 1 |
| 2017–2021 | 13.83 | 15.51 | 13.85 | 9.54 | 7 / 7 / 9 / 2 |

**Flex allocation** — moving the RB/WR split by ±0.10 moves the top 25 by one or two places.

| RB/WR | QB | RB | WR | TE | top-25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| .35/.55 | 24 | 32 | 49 | 14 | 7 / 7 / 10 / 1 |
| .45/.45 | 24 | 35 | 47 | 14 | 8 / 7 / 9 / 1 |
| .55/.35 | 24 | 37 | 44 | 14 | 9 / 7 / 8 / 1 |

**Depth weight** — nearly inert. Quadrupling it to 1.0 moves one place in the top 25 and never
lifts a below-replacement player above overall rank 103.

## 7. Regenerating the curve

```
npm run ingest -- --seasons 2023,2024,2025 --career-seasons 2017,…,2025 --db .local/curve.db
npm run generate:production-curves -- --db .local/curve.db
```

This rewrites `src/utility/productionCurve.generated.ts` and changes `curveVersion`, which every
board entry publishes as `productionCurveVersion`. A published value can always be traced back to
the evidence that produced it.
